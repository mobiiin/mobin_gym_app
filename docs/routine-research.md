# FORGE routine templates: design notes

These are original FORGE starting templates, not exercise programs tested as complete packages in these papers. They are intended for healthy adults and are editable, including the exercises, order, sets, rep targets, rest, and number of training days. Individual needs, equipment, movement tolerance, and recovery vary.

- The 2026 [ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/) recommends resistance training at least twice weekly for strength and identifies weekly volume as relevant to hypertrophy. Its overview reports that resistance training improves outcomes across many training prescriptions; the templates use conservative starting volumes, not a promise of a specific outcome.
- A 2024 [systematic review of full-body versus split routines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595233/) found no significant difference in strength or hypertrophy when weekly volume was matched. That supports offering both structures according to schedule and preference; it does not establish that all of these specific templates have equivalent volume.
- A 2026 [dose-response meta-regression](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/) found that higher weekly volume was associated with more muscle growth, with diminishing returns. The sets listed are starting suggestions. Some muscles receive indirect work from compound lifts, so avoid simply adding up every set as a full direct set for every muscle involved.
- A 2023 [network meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414459/) found that a broad range of resistance-training prescriptions improved outcomes; multiset programs performed well in the evidence analyzed.

## Using a template

Choose a schedule that can be repeated consistently. For two- and three-day full-body plans, leaving a rest day between sessions can help recovery. The 3-day push/pull/legs template trains each major area mainly once per week; it is a scheduling option, not a claim that this frequency is optimal. Four- and five-day options can be spread across the week with rest as needed. Optional exercises and cardio are extra, not required for the routine to count.

Select loads that allow controlled technique; build gradually and adjust volume when recovery, time, or experience calls for it. The displayed set, rep and rest values are editable targets, not automatic prescriptions or logged sets. For movements that hurt or are unsuitable, stop and substitute a comfortable alternative. Seek individualized guidance where appropriate.
